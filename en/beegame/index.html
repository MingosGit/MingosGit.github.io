BeeGame

A turn-based strategy game in Python that simulates the relationship between bees and human activity. It implements symbolic AI: Expectimax with chance nodes, A* search for pathfinding and a multi-component heuristic function. An academic project aimed at raising environmental awareness.

01

The artificial intelligence

Expectimax

Optimal decision making that accounts for probabilities: an extension of Minimax with chance nodes for the weather and flower reproduction.

A* Pathfinding

Optimal routes back to the hive avoiding obstacles, with a Manhattan distance heuristic.

Game theory

A MAX agent (the bee) against a MIN agent (humanity), with strategic pruning constraints in the decision tree.

Complex heuristic

An evaluation function with 7 weighted components and adjustable weights to assess every game state.

Chance events

A weather system (rain, sun, normal) and flower reproduction with dynamic probabilities every 4 turns.

3 game modes

Manual player, basic random AI or advanced Expectimax for both agents.

02

Game mechanics

The bee — MAX agent

  • Health: 100 points — it dies at 0
  • Energy: 100 points — spent when moving and collecting
  • Nectar: capacity of 50 units; the goal is to store it in the hive
  • Actions: move, pollinate flowers, rest and return to the hive with A*

Humanity — MIN agent

  • Pesticides: contaminate flowers — they die after 3
  • Obstacles: block cells — up to 4 at once with FIFO removal
  • Constraints: pesticides only within radius 2 of the bee; obstacles only within radius 3 of the hive

Weather — chance nodes every 4 turns

  • Rain (10 %): removes 1 pesticide from every flower
  • Sun (15 %): raises the flower reproduction probability to 40 %
  • Normal (75 %): no special effects

Why it exists

The game aims to raise awareness of how important bees are for pollination, showing interactively how pesticides and environmental obstacles affect their survival.

03

AI concepts applied

Informed search (A*), Minimax extended with probabilistic nodes (Expectimax), uncertainty modelling, strategic pruning and optimisation of heuristic functions with adjustable weights.
